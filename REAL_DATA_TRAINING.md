# CHAKRA-AI Real-Data-Calibrated XGBoost Training

## What changed
The earlier XGBoost decision engine trained on physically structured but broad synthetic ranges. This build keeps the same model role and UI, but calibrates the training generator to published real-world textile measurements and open LCA references.

## Real-world anchors used
1. **Carbonfact Open Source LCA Database for Footwear & Apparel v1.1.0 (2026)**
   - Open textile process LCA database covering spinning, weaving, dyeing, finishing, printing and textile assembly.
   - CHAKRA uses public stage-level GHG reference distributions (especially weaving and textile assembly) to calibrate stage-burden magnitudes.
   - License: CC BY-SA 4.0. Attribution: Data source: The Carbonfact Open Source LCA Database for Footwear & Apparel, Carrières, Vieira & Vandepaer (2026), DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21102755.

2. **Palamutcu, Energy 35(7), 2010, DOI 10.1016/j.energy.2010.03.029**
   - Actual plant measurements / monthly records used to calibrate electricity SEC distributions.
   - Spinning: 3.24-3.47 kWh/kg
   - Weaving: 1.58-2.24 kWh/kg
   - Wet processing: 0.79-1.05 kWh/kg
   - Clothing manufacturing: 0.065-0.195 kWh/kg

3. **Uddin et al., PLOS Sustainability and Transformation (2023), DOI 10.1371/journal.pstr.0000072**
   - Face-to-face survey of 18 textile wet-processing factories.
   - Dyehouse water average: 136 L/kg; observed factory range 28-285 L/kg.
   - Chemical use average: 449 g/kg; observed range 152-705 g/kg.
   - Individual factory rows are not public, so CHAKRA uses the published distribution statistics only.

4. **Central Electricity Authority CO2 Baseline Database v21.0**
   - Indian grid screening baseline already used by the deterministic LCA layer and now centers the ML training grid-factor distribution.

## Training design
- 12,000 hybrid training examples are generated by controlled bootstrapping/augmentation around the real published distributions above.
- Carbonfact stage GHG values are used through quantile calibration so stage magnitudes match open LCA reference distributions while preserving relationships to measurable CHAKRA inputs.
- LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH risk labels are derived from cohort percentiles of environmental/resource burden rather than the old arbitrary fixed thresholds.
- Priority-stage labels are derived from the greatest normalized stage burden in the real-data-calibrated cohort.

## Current internal holdout
- Risk classification holdout accuracy: generated at startup and exposed by the API.
- Priority-stage holdout accuracy: generated at startup and exposed by the API.

These are **internal hybrid-dataset holdout metrics, not external factory accuracy**. A separate pilot dataset from independent factories is still required to claim external real-world validation.

## Correct claim for presentations
Use:
> "CHAKRA-AI's XGBoost decision engine is trained on a real-world-data-calibrated hybrid textile dataset built from open textile LCA references and published factory measurements, with controlled augmentation for model training."

Do not use:
> "The model is trained purely on thousands of real factories."

## Why this is stronger
The model no longer learns from arbitrary uniform ranges. Its training population is constrained by published textile-industry measurements for energy, water and chemical use and by open process-level LCA reference distributions.
